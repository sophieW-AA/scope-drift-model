# PM Hiring Materials

Generate 4 Word documents from 5 inputs for any PM hire at Frontiers.

## Inputs

1. **Role title**
2. **Seniority level** — Associate PM, PM, Senior PM, or Principal PM
3. **Location**
4. **Domain description** — 5 bullet points about the business area
5. **Hiring committee** — names and titles

Invent all other details (metrics, investment amount, interview questions, signals, red flags) from the domain description.

## Outputs

1. **Job Requisition** (.docx) — post on LinkedIn / Workable
2. **Interview Process Guide** (.docx) — 3-stage plan for all interviewers
3. **Case Study** (.docx) — 1-page scenario for the candidate
4. **Cheat Sheet** (.docx) — evaluation guide for the case study panel

## Brand

Apply Frontiers brand to all documents:

- Logo + `#003BDE` divider at top of every document
- Headings: `#003BDE` or `#282828`, Arial/Calibri, Bold
- Body: `#545454`, Regular weight
- Captions: `#6B6B6B`
- Tables: `#F7F7F7` background
- Strong signals: `#32AE88` (Teal Green)
- Red flags: `#B92D2D`
- Voice: professional yet approachable, scientific yet accessible

## Document 1: Job Requisition

Use these exact sections:

1. **WHO WE ARE** — Use standard boilerplate: "At Frontiers, our purpose is simple yet ambitious: to make science open..."
2. **ABOUT THE ROLE** — 2–3 paragraphs. Transformational framing. Use "you will" not "the candidate will."
3. **KEY RESPONSIBILITIES** — 10–15 bullets. Strong verbs. Full PM lifecycle. Include AI/automation.
4. **REQUIREMENTS** — Split: Business expertise (4–5), Technical skills (4–5), Additional skills (3–4)
5. **BENEFITS** — Standard Frontiers list (wellbeing days, volunteering, EAP, learning hub)
6. **DIVERSITY STATEMENT** — Standard Frontiers text

## Document 2: Interview Process Guide

Label: **"INTERNAL — DO NOT SHARE WITH CANDIDATES"**

### Design Principles

- 3 stages after HR screening. Progression: WHO → HOW → WHAT
- Map to the 12 Frontiers PM competencies (see Competency Map below)
- Each competency assessed exactly once — zero overlap
- High agency detected at every stage as a cross-cutting lens, not a separate competency
- Each stage includes handoff notes for the next interviewer
- Calibrate depth to the seniority level being hired

### Competency Map

| Competency | Area | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| Product Vision & Roadmapping | Product Strategy | 1 — Hiring Manager |
| Strategic Impact | Product Strategy | 1 — Hiring Manager |
| Managing Up | Influencing People | 1 — Hiring Manager |
| Team Leadership | Influencing People | 1 — Hiring Manager |
| Feature Specification | Product Execution | 2 — Cross-functional |
| Product Delivery | Product Execution | 2 — Cross-functional |
| Stakeholder Management | Influencing People | 2 — Cross-functional |
| Quality Assurance | Product Execution | 2 — Cross-functional |
| Business Outcome Ownership | Product Strategy | 3 — Case Study |
| Fluency with Data | Customer Insight | 3 — Case Study |
| Voice of the Customer | Customer Insight | 3 — Case Study |
| User Experience Design | Customer Insight | 3 — Case Study |

Plus: Communication & Presence (Stage 3 only).

### Stage 1 — Hiring Manager (Head of Product) — 45 min

**Focus**: WHO is this person?

Assess: Product Vision & Roadmapping, Strategic Impact, Managing Up, Team Leadership.

**Agency lens**: Listen for "I decided to…" vs "I was told to…". Do they describe driving outcomes or coordinating activities?

Provide 5–7 domain-specific questions with strong/weak answer examples, follow-up probes, signals vs flags table, and scoring rubric.

End with handoff note: "Share assessment with Stage 2. Flag areas to probe deeper."

### Stage 2 — Cross-functional (PM / Stakeholder / EM) — 45 min

**Focus**: HOW do they work?

Assess: Feature Specification, Product Delivery, Stakeholder Management, Quality Assurance.

**Agency lens**: Listen for "I figured out…" vs "I escalated and waited…". Do they find workarounds or stall?

Provide 5–7 domain-specific questions with strong/weak answer examples, follow-up probes, signals vs flags table, and scoring rubric.

Include: "Adapt questions based on Stage 1 notes."

End with handoff note: "Brief the panel before the case study."

### Stage 3 — Case Study (Full Panel) — 45 min

**Focus**: WHAT would they do?

Assess: Business Outcome Ownership, Fluency with Data, Voice of the Customer, UX Design, Communication & Presence.

**Agency lens**: Part C is the ultimate agency test. High-agency candidates say exactly what data they need, from whom, how they'd get it, and what they'd do in the meantime. They use "when" language, not "if."

### Consolidated Scorecard

Include at end of document. All 13 competencies with Stage, Assessor, and Rating (Strong / Acceptable / Concern).

Add: **Overall Agency Assessment** — Game Changer (high talent + high agency) / Moderate (solid, coachable) / Low (concern).

Final recommendation: Hire / Don't hire / Discuss.

### High Agency — What to Listen For (all stages)

**Signals**: takes ownership without being asked, reframes "I can't because" into "how might I", treats being wrong as useful information, default assumption is "I can figure this out", uses "when we solve this" not "if we can solve this."

**Red flags**: blames former teams/tools/circumstances, says "that wasn't my job", accepts constraints without questioning, waits for perfect information, says "we're stuck" without proposing a path.

## Document 3: Case Study (1 page)

Must fit ONE page.

1. **Header**: Logo + `#003BDE` divider
2. **Title**: "[Role Title] — Case Study"
3. **Context**: One energetic paragraph about the domain
4. **Current State**: Table with 6–8 invented metrics. Below the table, state the total investment envelope (e.g. "You have a team budget of CHF 1.2M covering all team costs — new development, maintenance, and operations") and frame the core question: "Is it worth it?" `#F7F7F7` background.
5. **Format & Panel**: `#6B6B6B` text. "30-min presentation + 15-min Q&A. Present however works best." List panel from hiring committee.
6. **Your Brief**:
   - **Part A — Build the case**: Back-of-envelope business case, ROI, payback. "State your assumptions — we'll pressure-test them with you."
   - **Part B — What would you build?**: "Back every recommendation with data and a clear rationale. Ambition AND pragmatism. At least one idea beyond conventional thinking."
   - **Part C — What's missing?**: "What data, who to talk to, how to get it. Resourcefulness matters."
7. **Closing** (italic, `#6B6B6B`): "You won't have all the information — that's by design."

### Tone Rules

- "We're excited to see" not "you will be evaluated"
- "Show us how you'd approach" not "demonstrate your ability"
- "We'll pressure-test them with you" not "justify your recommendations"
- Supportive and exciting, never adversarial

## Document 4: Cheat Sheet

Label: **"INTERNAL — DO NOT SHARE WITH CANDIDATES"**

1. **What the exercise tests** — 5 dimensions mapped to competencies
2. **For each Part (A, B, C)**: what "good" looks like, signals (`#32AE88`) vs flags (`#B92D2D`) table, 2–3 probe questions
3. **Scoring rubric** — 5 dimensions, Strong / Acceptable / Concern
4. **Agency check** — For Part C, note whether candidate shows high-agency language
5. **Closing**: "A safe, polished presentation is less interesting than a bold, well-reasoned one with rough edges."

## Consistency Rules

- All 4 documents must align. Requisition describes competencies. Interview process assesses them. Case study tests the final set. Cheat sheet evaluates them.
- 12 Frontiers PM competencies + Communication & Presence = 13 total, each assessed exactly once.
- High agency is a lens across all stages, not a separate competency.
- No question or competency appears in more than one stage.
